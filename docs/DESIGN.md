# Design

How the app looks, moves and reads. How the UI code is built is in [FRONTEND.md](FRONTEND.md), and the theme setting is in [settings](product-specs/settings.md).

The tokens in the frontmatter above are read from `src/styles.css`, which stays the source of truth: when the two disagree, fix this doc. Color keys are `<theme>-<token>`, so `sakura-card-mark` is the `--card-mark` variable in Sakura's block. The components in the frontmatter show the default Tokyo Rain stock; every theme fills the same roles with its own tokens.

## Overview

**Creative North Star: "One deal, seven stocks"**

Every round deals four identical cards, and each anime genre prints them on its own card stock. The cards share one anatomy in every theme: an index mark in the top corner, the title at the foot, a frame, and a printed back that the reveal turns over. A theme is not a paint swap. It changes the stock, the frame, the index mark, the back, the display face, the buttons, the fields and the backdrop, and keeps the layout, sizes and order of play identical.

The game is played on a phone under time pressure, so expression never hides the options, the timer or the result. Each theme commits to a strict palette of a few colors and one way of showing a pick. Ornament is printed on the stock (rims, bands, stripes, screentone, rivets, foil) and drawn in CSS and small inline SVG; there are no raster assets. Motion is decoration that only runs when the player's motion setting allows it.

The seven themes, in picker order, with their ids in `src/prefs/prefs.ts`: Tokyo Rain (`tokyo-rain`, the default), Sakura (`sakura`), Shonen (`shonen`), Mecha (`mecha`), Magical Girl (`magical-girl`), Isekai (`isekai`) and Retro VHS (`retro-vhs`). Each player picks one on their device.

**Key Characteristics:**
- One card anatomy, seven printed stocks.
- The four options are always equal and never animate in.
- The right card turns over to its printed back at the reveal.
- A display face of its own for each theme, evened out with `font-size-adjust`.
- Depth is printed on the stock, not lifted off the page.
- Every text and graphic pair meets WCAG AA, checked by a test.

## Colors

Each theme is one block of variables on any element with `data-theme`, in two families: the chrome around the cards and the card stock itself.

### Primary
- **Accent** (`accent`, with `accent-ink` on it): the primary button, the timer's fill, the lit equalizer, focus outlines, the selected theme in the picker, the caret and text selection. Tokyo Rain's is signal amber, Sakura's vermilion, Shonen's spot red, Mecha's hazard yellow, Magical Girl's pink, Isekai's menu yellow, Retro VHS's sunset orange.

### Secondary
- **Card stock** (`card`, `card-ink`): the face of an open card.
- **Index mark** (`card-mark`, `card-mark-ink`): the index mark, the pick stamp, and in most themes the card's frame. The frame must hold 3:1 on the stock.
- **Chosen stock** (`card-chosen`, `card-chosen-ink`): the card this player picked.
- **Dimmed stock** (`card-dim`, with `card-muted` as its ink): a card that steps back, once this player has locked in or when it is not the answer. Its title stays readable at 4.5:1.
- **Alert** (`card-alert`): the strike through a wrong pick and the text of its stamp.
- **Printed back** (`card-back`, `card-back-ink`): the right card's back, and a face-down card.

### Tertiary
- **Right and wrong** (`good`, `bad`): verdict and error colors on the panels. They never carry meaning alone; an icon and words always go with them.
- **Material colors**, only where a theme's stock needs them: Sakura's `blossom` and gold `foil`, Magical Girl's `foil` and its `--foil-sheen` gradient, Retro VHS's three sunset stripes (`stripe-1` to `stripe-3`).

### Neutral
- **Page** (`page`): behind everything, under the theme's texture. Also the fill of fields.
- **Panel** and **raised** (`panel`, `raised`): the panels that hold each screen, and quiet buttons, chips and tracks on them.
- **Line** (`line`): dividers and quiet borders, and the equalizer's bars while the clip loads.
- **Edge** (`edge`): the rim of fields and choices, at 3:1 on the page and the panel.
- **Ink** and **muted** (`ink`, `muted`): body text and secondary text.
- Sakura's `wave` is the seigaiha pattern, one shade off its page.

### Contrast the tests enforce
`src/themes.test.ts` reads every theme block and fails below these ratios:
- Text at 4.5:1: `ink` on `page`, `panel` and `raised`; `muted` on `page`, `panel` and `raised`; `accent-ink` on `accent`; `bad` on `page` and `panel`; `card-ink` on `card`; `card-muted` on `card` and on `card-dim`; `card-chosen-ink` on `card-chosen`; `card-back-ink` on `card-back`; `card-mark-ink` on `card-mark`; `card-alert` on `card`.
- Graphics at 3:1: `edge` on `page` and `panel`; `accent` on `page` and `panel`; `good` on `panel`; `bad` on `panel`; `card-mark` on `card`.

A new color role that carries text or a boundary gets a pair here in the same change.

### Named Rules
**The Solid Ground Rule.** Text always sits on a solid token color, never on a texture, a stripe or a gradient.

**The Card Stock Rule.** Cards take only `card-*` tokens; the chrome around them takes only the page and panel tokens. A card reads the same wherever it sits.

**The One Grammar Rule.** Each theme shows a pick one way, in its own material, and every chosen card in that theme uses it.

## Typography

**Display font:** each theme's own, self-hosted, Latin only, OFL licensed (see the frontmatter's `<theme>-display` entries).
**Title font:** the face of the card titles, the stamp and the back's title (`<theme>-title`). Mecha uses the system sans in bold capitals and Isekai uses Pixelify Sans; the other five reuse their display face.
**Body font:** the system sans. Japanese titles (`lang="ja"`) use the device's Japanese fonts (`--ja-font`) wherever they appear, never a display face's fallback.

**Character:** the display face is the theme's voice: a station gothic, a woodblock antique, a manga letterer, a hangar stencil, a pop rounded face, a pixel font, a tape counter. The body stays plain so the long text reads the same everywhere.

Per theme, in the variables `--display-*` and `--title-*`: the font, weight, case (Shonen, Mecha and Retro VHS set capitals), tracking and `font-size-adjust` (`--display-adjust`, `--title-adjust`). The adjust evens out the faces' very different x-heights, so a heading keeps its size from Tokyo Rain's gothic to Isekai's pixels.

### Hierarchy
- **Home title** (display face, 3rem, 3.75rem from 640 px): the game's name.
- **Countdown** (display face, 3.75rem, tabular figures): the seconds before the clip.
- **Bill lead** (display face, 2.75rem, 4rem from 640 px, line height 1.05): the winner's name on the results bill.
- **Verdict** (display face, 1.875rem, line height 1.25): the reveal's first line, with its icon.
- **Headline** (display face, 1.5rem): "Round 3 of 15", the answer's title at the reveal, panel titles at 1.25rem.
- **Card title** (title face, 1rem, 1.25rem from 640 px, line height 1.3): the option's title, and the back's title at the same size.
- **Body** (system sans, 1rem) and **small** (0.875rem, muted): instructions, the scoring line, stats.
- **Stamp** (title face, 0.75rem): the pick stamp, and the back's label and meta line.
- Buttons use the display face at the body size (primary) or small size (quiet).

### Named Rules
**The Own Face Rule.** Headings, the countdown, the verdict, the bill's names, buttons and the index mark are set in the theme's display face through the `.display` class or the display variables, never a hard-coded family.

**The Japanese Face Rule.** Japanese text drops the display face, its case, its tracking and its size adjust, and takes `--ja-font`.

## Layout

Mobile first: every screen works one-handed from 360 px wide and scales up to desktop. One breakpoint changes the cards: from 40rem (640 px) the cards double their height and their titles grow.

- **The round's stage** (`Stage.tsx`): a slot above the cards, the cards, and the lines below them. On a phone the stage fills the first screen (the viewport height less 15rem) and pushes the cards to the bottom, within thumb reach. The slot keeps one height, so the cards sit in the same place from the face-down deal through the answer.
- **The cards** sit in a 2x2 grid with 0.75rem gaps, each half the width. A card is at least 6rem tall on a phone and 11.5rem from 640 px, and cards in a row share one height whatever their titles.
- **The listening panel and the timer strip** fill the slot during a round. The countdown fills it before the clip starts.
- **Panels** hold each screen, padded 1rem (1.5rem from 640 px).
- **The reveal** lays out its own column with 1.25rem gaps; see the reveal under Components.
- **Chrome that stays in reach:** the Preferences button is on every screen, and the lobby's start button stays in view at the bottom of the screen with the count of matching songs and anime, however long the settings form gets.
- The keys hint ("Keys 1 to 4 work too.") shows only where a fine pointer suggests a keyboard.

### Named Rules
**The Fixed Deal Rule.** Nothing moves a card once dealt: the stamp sits outside the faces, the back prints its title at the front's size, and Isekai reserves the cursor's gutter on every card.

## Elevation & Depth

Depth is printed, not lifted. Stocks show their edges with inset rims, bands and rings drawn in `box-shadow: inset`, outlines, pseudo-elements and clip paths. The only drop shadow is the panel's soft ambient shadow (`--panel-shadow`), which sets panels off the page texture; Shonen has none.

### Shadow Vocabulary
- **Panel shadow** (`--panel-shadow`, for example Tokyo Rain's `0 1.25rem 2.5rem -1.5rem rgb(0 0 0 / 0.8)`): a soft shadow below each panel. Sakura tints its shadow vermilion.
- **Printed rim** (`inset 0 0 0 <n>px <color>`): Sakura's double rims, the muted index mark's outline, the missed stamp's ring.
- **Line band** (`inset 0 -0.625rem 0 var(--card-mark)`): Tokyo Rain's station band along a card's foot, and its 3px version on quiet buttons and the panel's top.
- **Window ring** (`0 0 0 2px #05060f`): Isekai's dark ring round its white rims, and Isekai's panel shadow. It has no offset.

### Named Rules
**The Print Not Lift Rule.** A rim, band or ring is drawn on the stock. No hard offset shadows; the only lift is an open card rising 2px under a hovering pointer.

## Shapes

Each theme resizes three radii: `lg` for buttons, fields, index marks, the stamp and the equalizer's bar tops; `xl` for card faces; `2xl` for panels. The corners follow the stock: Shonen and Mecha are square or nearly, Sakura and Retro VHS are cut close, Magical Girl is soft and its buttons are pills.

Signature silhouettes:
- **Mecha's chamfer:** panels and card faces lose their four corners to a clip path (0.875rem on panels, 0.625rem on cards).
- **Shonen's round index** and **Magical Girl's diamond gem** break the square mark.
- **Magical Girl's four-point sparkle** appears on a chosen card, on the radio dot and in the backdrop.
- **Isekai's triangle cursor** marks the chosen item and the checked radio.

## Components

### The card
The card is the system. `OptionCard.tsx` renders it; `.card` and its faces in `src/styles.css` style it.

- **Anatomy:** a front face with the index mark (the option's number, 1 to 4) in the top corner and the title at the foot; a back face printed only on the right card at the reveal. Padding 0.75rem 0.875rem 1rem, corners at the theme's `xl` radius, a 2px `card-mark` frame by default, titles in the title face.
- **Focus:** a 3px accent outline, offset 4px.
- **Hover:** only an open card, only where the pointer can hover: it rises 2px in 150ms.

States, from `data-state`:
- **Face down:** before the clip starts the four cards lie as blank backs, not turned and hidden from assistive technology. Nothing is printed on them.
- **Open:** the card stock, answerable by tap, click or keys 1 to 4.
- **Chosen:** this player's pick. The chosen stock, the pick stamp "Your pick", and the theme's selection grammar.
- **Muted:** the dimmed stock (`card-dim` with `card-muted`). The other cards once this player has locked in, and every card that is not the answer at the reveal. The index mark turns to an outline and the frame dims, and the title stays readable.
- **Missed:** this player's wrong pick at the reveal. The stock stays, the title is struck through in `card-alert` (2px), and the stamp reads "Your pick" with a cross.
- **Right:** the answer, turned over to its printed back: a label with a check ("Right answer", or "Right answer, your pick"), the title in the title face at the front's size, and a meta line such as "OP 1 · 2013". Screen readers hear "(the right answer)" after the title.

### The pick stamp
A small tag across the card's top edge, 0.5rem from the right, outside the faces so the card keeps its height. The index mark's colors, the title face at 0.75rem, the theme's `lg` radius, and a 0.875rem icon when it carries one. On a missed pick it inverts to the card stock with `card-alert` text inside a 2px `card-alert` ring. Shonen letters a chosen pick's stamp in its spot red with an ink ring, tilted 3 degrees.

### Buttons
- **Shape:** the theme's `lg` radius (Magical Girl: a pill), printed in the theme's display face.
- **Primary:** the accent fill with `accent-ink`, padded 0.625rem 1rem; it brightens 10% under the pointer.
- **Quiet:** secondary actions, a size smaller (0.875rem, padded 0.5rem 0.75rem) so a phone's header holds them on one line in every face. The raised fill with a line border by default; each stock prints its own frame (table above).
- **Focus:** a 2px accent outline, offset 2px. **Disabled:** half opacity and a not-allowed cursor.
- An action that affects others or can't be undone asks once more with a quiet Yes and No.

### Inputs / Fields
- **Style:** the page color inside a rim of `edge` (`--field-rim`: 1px by default, 2px in Shonen, 3px in Isekai), at the `lg` radius. Selects draw their chevron in the theme's ink.
- **Checkboxes and radios** sit on the same field: a check on the accent when checked, an accent dot for a radio. Magical Girl's dot is a sparkle; Isekai's radios are square with a cursor.
- **Focus:** a 2px accent outline, offset 1px (2px on checkboxes and radios).

### Panels
The panel fill, a 1px line border, the `2xl` radius and the panel shadow; each stock reframes it (table above). A panel title is in the display face at 1.25rem.

### The listening panel
`Listening.tsx` fills the slot above the cards with the one thing the game is about, the music, and tells "I can't hear it" apart from "I don't know it".
- A row of equalizer bars from a fixed skyline, the same on every device: 14 on a phone, 24 from 640 px, 3.5rem to 11rem tall.
- **Loading:** the bars rest low in the line color. **Ready:** lit in the theme's color at a little under half height. **Playing:** full height, and moving with motion on. **Failed:** flat.
- Words below in small muted type say the same thing, as a status: "Loading the clip…", "The clip is ready.", "Listen.", or that the clip didn't load and the player can still answer with no penalty. The bars are decoration; the words carry the state.
- Below it, the timer strip: a 0.5rem raised track whose accent fill shrinks linearly, and the seconds left.

### The reveal
The reveal runs in a fixed order:
1. **The verdict first,** under the round's heading: a check or cross and the words, in the display face at 1.875rem. A right answer shows its points; a wrong or missing one earns "You skipped the OP?!" (or the ED). A skipped round says nobody scores. Below it, this player's place, total and streak in small muted type.
2. **Then the cards,** in their order: the right one turned over to its back, this player's wrong pick missed, the rest muted.
3. **Then the answer:** after a line, the cover (6rem by 9rem at the `lg` radius), the title in the display face at 1.5rem, the other titles muted, the song, the artists and the season.
4. **Then the billed lineup:** the standings as a compact bill, the leader's name and score in the display face at 1.5rem, second and third larger than the rest, each with a check and "right", a cross and "wrong", or "no answer", and the points won or lost.
5. A line saying what comes next.

### The results bill
The final standings billed like a festival lineup (`.bill-*`). Each row is a grid of place, name and score in tabular figures, rows split by a 1px line. The winner headlines: place and score on the first line, the full name across the width below it in the display face (bill lead). Second and third are billed below in the display face at 1.5rem with scores at 1.125rem; everyone else is packed smaller in the body face. Each name carries a small muted stats line: right answers, average time and best streak. This player's row carries a "you" chip.

### The theme picker
In the Preferences panel, each choice previews its own stock inside the page's theme: its page texture, a small card in its `card` and `card-mark`, and its name in its display face, framed in `line`, or `accent` when chosen. The previews use only variables, so they work inside another theme.

### Motion
Motion is decoration. It never carries information, and it runs only when the page's `data-motion` is `full`: the player's motion setting, or else the device's `prefers-reduced-motion`. Reduced motion stops every animation and every transition.

| What | Timing |
| :-- | :-- |
| The right card turning over | 700ms, `cubic-bezier(0.2, 0.7, 0.2, 1)`, after 200ms |
| The verdict and the answer rising in | 350ms, `cubic-bezier(0.16, 1, 0.3, 1)` |
| The countdown's tick | 300ms, the same curve |
| An open card lifting under the pointer | 150ms transition, the same curve |
| The equalizer while playing | 850ms, alternating, each bar offset |
| Isekai's cursor | a 1s blink |
| Retro VHS's home title | a 6s flicker |
| Backdrops | rain 0.6 to 1.3s, petals 9 to 16s, twinkles 2.4 to 4.8s, pixel stars 0.9 to 2.2s, Mecha's sweep 7s, the tracking band 9s; Shonen none |

Without motion the right card is shown already turned, and the rest shows at once.

**The Equal Four Rule.** The four options are the same stock, size and index mark, and they never animate in: they lie face down until the clip starts, then show at once, so every player can read them the moment the music plays.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do:
- **Do** put every new color in the theme blocks of `src/styles.css`, in all seven themes, and add its contrast pair to `src/themes.test.ts` when it carries text or a boundary.
- **Do** print a new component on the theme's stock: its tokens, its radii, its display face.
- **Do** give every state a shape, a word or an icon as well as a color.
- **Do** keep the cards where they were dealt: nothing added to a card may change its size.
- **Do** start every animation from a `motion-*` class or a theme rule under `data-motion='full'`.

### Don't:
- **Don't** use a gradient as a wash, glow or fill for its own sake. Gradients here draw a material: hard-stop stripes, foil sheen, screentone, scanlines, a page texture, a raindrop's streak.
- **Don't** use hard offset shadows. Rims, bands and rings are drawn inset or with no offset.
- **Don't** animate the options in, or make one option larger, brighter or earlier than the others.
- **Don't** use official artwork, logos, character names or fonts from One Piece, Naruto, Dragon Ball or any other series. The stocks evoke genres with original shapes and open fonts.
- **Don't** put text on a texture, a stripe or a foil.
- **Don't** show right and wrong in green and red alone.
- **Don't** autoplay audio before the player's first tap, and don't use emoji in the interface.

## UI copy
- Short, plain English in sentence case. The only exclamation marks are in the game's name and the playful reveal line: a wrong or missing answer earns "You skipped the OP?!" (or the ED) ([product sense](PRODUCT_SENSE.md)).
- Errors say what happened and what to do next: "That code doesn't match a lobby. Check it with the host." Never just "Error".

## Accessibility
- Every theme meets WCAG AA contrast: 4.5:1 for text, 3:1 for icons, field borders, card frames and focus outlines. `src/themes.test.ts` checks the pairs listed under Colors, and axe checks every screen in every theme (`e2e/a11y.spec.ts`).
- Right and wrong answers are shown with an icon and text, never by color alone.
- The game works with the keyboard alone, and screen readers get labeled controls. Decoration (the index marks, the backs, the equalizer, the backdrop) is hidden from them.
- Audio is the quiz itself, so it has no text alternative. The reveal gives the answer in text.
- Volume is adjustable at any time, starting at 15%.
